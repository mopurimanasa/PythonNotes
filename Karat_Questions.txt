12. 
John misses his bus and has to walk all his way from home to school. The distance between his school and home is D units. 
He starts his journey with an initial energy of K units. His energy decreases by 1 unit for every unit of distance walked. On his way to school,
there are N juice stalls. Each stall has a specific amount of juice in liters. His energy increases by 1 unit for every liter of juice he consumes. 
Note that in order to keep him walking, he should have non-zero energy. Write an algorithm to help John figure out the minimum number of juice stalls at which he should 
stop to successfully reach the school. In case he can't reach the school, the output will be -1.

13.

In a car racing game, the participating cars must be registered online prior to the game.

A car is assigned a registration number that is stored in a database. The registration number consists of digits from 0–9.

The registration number can be positive or negative.

A negative registration number denotes that the car is already registered online.
A positive registration number denotes that the car is a newly registered car.

Before the game starts, the system automatically assigns a track number to each car.

The track number is the smallest permutation of the car's registration number and never starts with zero.

Write an algorithm to generate the track number.

Given the IDs of the bus stations that have a bus operating between them, write an algorithm to help the administration find the distance covered by all the city buses.

15) 

Martin’s father goes for a jog every morning. Martin follows him several minutes later.

His father starts at a position that is X1 meters away from their home and runs in a straight line at a constant speed of V1 meters per step for N steps.

Martin is standing at a position X2 meters away from his home. He wonders how fast he must run, at a constant speed of V2 meters per step, to maximize F, 
where F is the number of his father’s footsteps (step positions) that Martin will land on during his run.

It is given that the first step Martin lands on from his starting position has already been landed on by his father.

If more than one possible value of V2 results in the same maximum number of common steps, choose the highest possible velocity as V2.

Write an algorithm to help Martin calculate F and V2.

16) 

In an organization, there are N employees, with employee IDs from 1 to N, working in different teams.

Each employee has a good relationship with their fellow team members. Each employee is assigned an integer X, which represents that employee's efficiency.

The sum of the efficiencies of all employees currently in the organization represents the organization's reputation.

Edwin is appointed as the manager of the organization for Q days. Edwin fires one employee each day.

When an employee is fired, K colleagues of that employee resign in protest. These K colleagues are the employees with the lowest efficiencies among the remaining members of the fired employee's team.

Kevin, the head of the database management system, must update the organization's reputation at the end of each day.

Write an algorithm to determine the reputation of the organization at the end of each day for Q days.

17) The manager of a supermarket wishes to hold an event in which he will distribute gift baskets to lucky customers.

There are N types of products, and each product has a price.

The gift baskets will be awarded to customers who pick a product pair whose difference in price is equal to a given integer value K.

Write an algorithm to help the manager find the total number of lucky customers who will win a gift basket.

18) 

A space organization has N satellites in orbit. The satellites are assigned IDs from 0 to N−1.

A satellite can transfer data to any other satellite that lies within its bandwidth range.

A team at the organization is working on a satellite communication project. They want to transfer data from the main satellite, with ID 0, to all the other satellites.

Because transferring data requires a large amount of power, a satellite can transfer data to only one other satellite at a time.

After receiving the data, that satellite can connect to the organization's server, which helps it connect to other satellites near it.

Only a fixed number of satellites can be within the bandwidth range of a satellite at a time.

Write an algorithm to determine the minimum number of iterations of data transfer required to connect all N satellites.
